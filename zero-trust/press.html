The game

Zero Trust is a puzzle game about a job few people know exists: physical penetration testing. Companies hire you to break into their own buildings, reach the server and walk out unseen, so they can learn how a real intruder would do it.

Each of its 56 jobs is a building, or a whole fenced site of them, seen from above in pixel art. Rooms stay dark until you step into them, so every job is read one door at a time: guards walk their rounds, cameras sweep, presence sensors bring the guards, and the receptionist decides who gets in.

You pick locks with a timing game, talk your way past the front desk by choosing who you are and whose name you drop, and read sticky notes and unlocked screens for passwords and schedules. Every job hides side findings, the kind of thing a real tester puts in the report, and they earn the stars that buy gear.

The jobs grow with the client: small businesses, mid-size companies, large corporations, critical infrastructure such as power stations, global conglomerates and finally multi-building complexes patrolled outside. The first job teaches by playing it, in the tester's own dry voice.

What is in it

  • 56 jobs for six kinds of client
  • Four stars a job: finish it, never be noticed, bring back a side finding, bring back all of them
  • Night jobs, day jobs with the staff around, and jobs at closing time
  • Buildings with several floors, elevators and air ducts
  • Lock picking, pretexts at the front desk, cloned badges, looped cameras, jammed sensors
  • Checkpoints, so a ten-minute job is not lost on its last floor
  • Lock assist, for anyone who wants more time on the locks
  • 69 Game Center achievements, a dozen of them surprises
  • Played with one finger, in portrait
  • No tracking, and the whole game plays offline
